# Chebyshev Filter
You cannot have $R_s$ and $R_L$ equal in even order Chebyshev filters.

Design a 3-Pole 0.5dB Chebyshev filter with a $f_c$ of 155 MHz with $R_s$ and $R_L$ both at 50$\ohm$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{First we calculate the Ref values} \\
C_{ref} = \frac{1}{2 \pi R_0 f_c} = \frac{1}{2 \pi 50 \times 155 \times 10^6} = 20.5\text{pF} \\ 
L_{ref} = \frac{R_0}{2\pi f_c} = \frac{50}{2 \pi 155 \times 10^6} = 51.4\text{nH} \\
\\
\text{Calculate normalized values for filter} \\
C_1 = C_{ref} \times 1.864 = 38.3\text{pF} \\
L_2 = L_{ref} \times 1.280 = 65.7\text{nH} \\
C_3 = C_{ref} \times 1.864 = 38.3\text{pF} \\
\end{aligned}
$$

We want to measure the ratio of power delivered to the available power

What is available power -- $P_{av}$
- Power that would be transferred when the load is matched to the source
- $V_s$ must be RMS

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_l = \frac{V_s}{2} \\
P_{av} = \frac{(\frac{V_s}{2})^2}{R_l} = \frac{(V_s)^2}{4R_l} \\
\end{aligned}
$$
Insertion Loss is the ratio in decibles
$$
\begin{aligned}
IL = 10 log_{10}(\frac{P_d}{P_{av}}) \\
\\
\text{where} \\
\\
P_{av} = \frac{(V_s)^2}{4R_l}\\
P_d = \frac{(V_s)^2}{R_l} \\
\\
\text{as such} \\
\\
IL_{dB} = 10 log_{10}4(\frac{V_l}{V_s})^2 \\
\end{aligned}
$$
# Available Power & Delivered Power
Typical circuits consist of a source, a load, and a network
![[Pasted image 20250924112218.png]]

This network could be a filter, amp, attenuator, etc.
You want to deliver some amount of power into the load impedance ($R_l$), which could be an antenna, resistor, attenuator, etc...

Question is how does the RF Circuit network affect our ability to deliver power

The voltage, current, or power source has some source impedance

To deliver maximum power from the source to the load, the load impedance must be the complex conjugate of the source impedance. 
![[Pasted image 20250924112607.png]]

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_s = R_s + jX_s \\
Z_L = R_L - jX_L \\
\end{aligned}
$$
For a matched load the reactance's are equal but opposite sign and the resistances are equal. The voltage across the load resistance is $V_s/2$

##### Available power 
is the maximum power that a source can deliver
- this requires a matched load $Z_L = Z_s^*$

##### Delivered Power
is the amount of power actually delivered to the load
- It can be less than the available power if there is a mismatch

**Mismatched resistances example**
$V_s$ must be in RMS
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_L = V_s\frac{R_L}{R_s + R_L} \\
P_L = \frac{V_s^2}{R_L} \\
P_L = V_s^2(\frac{R_L}{(R_s + R_L)^2})
\end{aligned}
$$
For maximum power transfer calculate the power, take the derivate w.r.t. $R_l$ and set to 0
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_L &= \frac{V_L^2}{R_L} \quad\quad V_L = \frac{R_L}{R_S + R_L} \\
P_L &= V_i \frac{R_L}{(R_S + R_L)^2} \\
\frac{dP_L}{dR_L} &= V_i \left[ R_L \left( \frac{-2}{(R_S + R_L)^3} \right) + \frac{1}{(R_S + R_L)^2} \right] \\
\frac{dP_L}{dR_L} &= V_i \left( \frac{R_S - R_L}{(R_S + R_L)^3} \right) = 0 \\
R_L &= R_S \quad \text{For max power transfer}
\end{aligned}
$$
