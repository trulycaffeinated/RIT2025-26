## Matching Networks
We need to find the series reactance that transforms the series resistance into the desired parallel resistance
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q = \frac{X_L}{R_S} \\
\\
R_P = (Q^2+1)R_S \\
\\
X_P = \frac{R_P}{Q} = (1+\frac{1}{Q^2})X_S
\end{aligned}
$$

Add an inductor in series, and you can pick that inductor (at a frequency of interest) to convert the 100$\ohm$ source impedance to a 1000$\ohm$ impedance

![[Pasted image 20250929110846.png]]
Find the required Q
$$
\begin{aligned}
R_P = R_S(Q^2+1) \quad \text{Converting $R_s$ to $R_p$} \\
\\
Q=\sqrt{\frac{1000}{100}-1} = 3 \\
\end{aligned}
$$
Calculate series inductor
$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= \frac{X_{LS}}{R_s} \\
X_{LS} &= Q \times R_s \\
X_{LS} &= 3 \times 100\Omega = 300 \\
\\
L_s &= \frac{X_{LS}}{2\pi f} = \frac{300}{2\pi \times 100e6} = 477\,nH
\end{aligned}
$$
Find the equivalent parallel inductor (used to calculate $C_P$)
$$
\begin{aligned}
X_{Lp} &= X_{Ls}\left( 1 + \frac{1}{Q^2} \right) \\
\\
\text{factor out $2\pi f$} \\
L_p &= L_s\left( 1 + \frac{1}{Q^2} \right) \\
\\
L_p &= 477\,\text{nH}\left( 1 + \frac{1}{3^2} \right) = 531\,\text{nH}
\end{aligned}
$$
Finally resonate the $L_p$ with a capacitor at the frequency of interest
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_P = \frac{1}{(2\pi f)^2L_p} = \frac{1}{(2\pi(100MHz))^2 \times 531nH} = 4.8pF
\end{aligned}
$$
